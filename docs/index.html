The turbolapper method

turbolapper is a Mac-native local analytics app from Adviser Labs. You drop in a data file on your Mac. The app profiles the file, builds questions from it, and turns the answers into a slide deck.

The AI runs on the machine, so the dataset stays on the Mac, and follow-up questions stay on a flat monthly or annual price. People who have hit the limit of Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets use it for files with millions of rows. Public demos go further, including more than 200 million NYC taxi rows on an M2 Mac with 32 GB.

How a data study is done

A data study in turbolapper moves through five steps: question, data, local analysis on your Mac, findings, and publish.

  1. Question

    Write the decision the study has to answer in one sentence. turbolapper also builds questions from the file you drop in. Keep the questions that serve the decision.

  2. Data

    Put the file on the Mac. Record the source, the geography, and the year. This repository documents a public county corpus: BEA county GDP (2001 to 2024), BEA personal income (1969 to 2024), IRS Statistics of Income (2015 to 2022), and Census population and migration (2020 to 2024). The year those four sources overlap is 2022.

  3. Local analysis on your Mac

    Prepare clean names, joins, and documented views, then ask the questions in turbolapper. The dataset stays on the Mac. turbolapper AFM uses the Apple Foundation Model already on the machine: macOS 27 with Apple Intelligence, 8 GB of memory (16 GB recommended), Apple M1 or later, a 66.2 MB download. turbolapper Medium runs on macOS 26 or later with 16 GB and ships a 3.15 GB model inside the app. turbolapper Heavy is coming soon for Macs with 48 GB or more, an M3 Max or better, and macOS 26 or later. Read the public demos on the turbolapper technology page.

  4. Findings

    Keep the claims a reader can check. Answers are built to show their work, with confidence labeled Verified, Estimated, or Directional. In this county bundle, a boolean flag is NULL when the underlying series is missing.

  5. Publish

    Leave with the slide deck. Cite the sources, the year, and the questions. Name the questions the tables still leave open. For the county bundle, those gaps are listed in Known Unknowns.

A public corpus in this repository

The reference docs here describe a US county economics bundle of about 3,200 counties. The wide table semantic.county_all_flags covers 3,253 counties and county-equivalents, one row per county per year. The join key is a zero-padded 5-digit FIPS code plus the year. The example study outline is one way to turn that corpus into a study, with the findings left for a run in turbolapper.
